Housing Finance and the Dynamic Transmission of Monetary Policy

This paper compares monetary transmission through housing valuation, home-purchase financing, and rate-and-term refinancing. Refinancing activity falls quickly after monetary tightening, but estimated cumulative contributions to the three-year consumption decline are larger for valuation and purchase financing. These findings show why refinancing activity alone is an incomplete gauge of the housing channel.

Data, methods, and findings

I combine mortgage originations by loan purpose with U.S. macroeconomic data for 1998–2023 in an eight-variable Bayesian structural VAR with stochastic volatility. A first-entry path decomposition compares the three margins on a common consumption scale.

In the baseline model, posterior medians indicate larger cumulative contributions from valuation- and purchase-entry paths than from refinancing-entry paths. I assess posterior uncertainty and sensitivity to identifying restrictions, distinguishing the speed of mortgage-market adjustment from its cumulative contribution to spending.

Labor Market Slack, Refinancing Eligibility, and the Distribution of Monetary Transmission

Employment-based refinancing eligibility can exclude unemployed homeowners from monetary easing precisely when liquidity is most valuable to them. In a heterogeneous-household model with illiquid housing and labor-market-dependent unemployment risk, this exclusion delays rather than eliminates their gains from a rate cut. The delay hinges on housing illiquidity.

Model and empirical evidence

The model combines long-term mortgages, illiquid housing, and a frictional labor market, calibrated to CPS labor flows, Freddie Mac mortgage rates, and SCF liquid assets. In model simulations, employment verification delays refinancing by one to two quarters for unemployed homeowners.

I examine the mechanism using SCF, PSID, and HMDA data. Families with unemployment spells were about half as likely to refinance in 2009. Across 386 metropolitan areas, the refinancing shortfall in high-unemployment areas was concentrated in applications rather than denials. This evidence is consistent with the eligibility mechanism.

Mortgage Refinancing Waves and Heterogeneous Interest-Rate Transmission

This project studies why similar mortgage-rate declines generate different refinancing waves. Using a Freddie Mac panel covering 72.8 million loan-months, I distinguish loan-pool composition, refinancing incentives, and borrower responses, and examine whether a common conditional response function can explain activity across episodes.

Current empirical work: mortgage prepayment and refinancing incentives

I construct loan-specific refinancing incentives and benchmark monthly voluntary payoff models on a 2017 origination cohort comprising 1.66 million loan-months. With chronological training, validation, and test splits, XGBoost reduces out-of-time log loss by 7.1% relative to L2-regularized logit on 377,729 test loan-months.

This benchmark predicts broad voluntary payoff, which includes refinancing and home-sale-related repayment. It does not yet isolate pure refinancing. The improvement in individual probability scores does not translate into lower monthly payoff-count errors, a distinction that matters for the project's micro-to-aggregate question.
