Research

Job Market Paper

Housing Finance and the Dynamic Transmission of Monetary Policy

Read paper (PDF)

This paper compares the consumption contributions of housing valuation, home-purchase financing, and rate-and-term refinancing using purpose-specific mortgage data, a Bayesian structural VAR with stochastic volatility, and a dynamic path decomposition. Refinancing contracts rapidly after monetary tightening, yet valuation and purchase financing make larger estimated cumulative contributions to the consumption decline over three years. These findings imply that refinancing activity alone is an incomplete gauge of the housing channel, overlooking the more persistent consumption effects transmitted through housing valuation and home purchases.

Working Paper

Labor Market Slack, Refinancing Eligibility, and the Distribution of Monetary Transmission

Employment-based refinancing eligibility can exclude unemployed homeowners from monetary easing precisely when liquidity is most valuable to them. I develop a heterogeneous-household model with long-term fixed-rate mortgages, illiquid housing, and labor-market-dependent unemployment risk, calibrated to CPS labor flows and mortgage-rate dynamics. I show that eligibility restrictions delay rather than eliminate unemployed homeowners' gains from a rate cut. In the model, this delay disappears when housing is liquid, while the value of restoring refinancing access depends on whether it permits equity extraction.

Work in Progress

Mortgage Refinancing Waves and Heterogeneous Interest-Rate Transmission

This project studies why similar declines in mortgage rates generate different refinancing waves. Using a Freddie Mac mortgage panel, it develops a micro-to-aggregate framework that distinguishes the composition of outstanding mortgages, loan-specific refinancing incentives, and borrowers' responses to those incentives. A central test is whether a common conditional response function can explain refinancing activity across episodes after accounting for differences in loan-pool composition and incentives. The project examines how these margins shape aggregate refinancing waves and the distribution of potential payment relief across borrowers.
