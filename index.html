Housing Finance and the Dynamic Transmission of Monetary Policy

Which housing-finance margins carry monetary policy into consumption? I compare housing valuation, home-purchase financing, and refinancing within a common framework.

Abstract

Using mortgage originations by loan purpose and U.S. macroeconomic data for 1998–2023, I estimate a Bayesian structural VAR with stochastic volatility and apply a first-entry path decomposition. Refinancing activity falls quickly after monetary tightening. In the baseline model, posterior medians suggest that valuation- and purchase-entry paths contribute more to the three-year consumption decline than refinancing-entry paths. Comparing all three margins on a common consumption scale distinguishes the speed of financing adjustment from its cumulative contribution to spending.

Labor Market Slack, Refinancing Eligibility, and the Distribution of Monetary Transmission

How does unemployment affect access to mortgage relief? I study how employment-based refinancing eligibility changes who benefits from lower mortgage rates, and when.

Abstract

Refinancing typically requires documented employment. In a calibrated household model with illiquid housing, I examine how eligibility rules shape the timing and distribution of gains from a mortgage-rate cut. When housing is sufficiently illiquid, exclusion delays most of the gain rather than removing it, household by household. A slack labor market delays the aggregate response more than it reduces it. Evidence from two household panels on refinancing during unemployment is consistent with the mechanism.

Work in progress

Mortgage Refinancing Waves and Heterogeneous Interest-Rate Transmission

Why do similar mortgage-rate declines generate different refinancing waves? I separate mortgage-pool composition, refinancing incentives, and borrower responses.

Project summary

Using Freddie Mac loan-level data, this project examines whether a common conditional response function can explain refinancing activity across episodes after accounting for differences in loan-pool composition and incentives. It studies how these margins shape aggregate refinancing waves and the distribution of potential payment relief across borrowers.
